# 02: History

Dating: **YL** = Years of the Lantern, counted from the founding of Solenne (0 YL). Earlier ages are dated roughly, "before the Lantern" (BL). Scholars disagree about anything older than about 2000 BL; codex entries should show that disagreement.

## The ages at a glance

| Age | Span | Defined by | How it ended |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Age of Sparks** | mythic | the Forging, the gods walking the world | Orr's death; the gods withdraw into their domains |
| **First Age: Age of Iron** | ~? to ~3000 BL | the **Orrin** giants and their **Wrought Dominion** | **The Rusting** |
| **Second Age: Age of Leaves** | ~3000 to ~1100 BL | the **Aelthi** courts in the great forests | **The Sundering of Ys** |
| **Third Age: Age of Ash** | ~1100 to ~400 BL | the **Ashen Kings of Qadir**; human kingdoms rise | **The Burning of Qadir** |
| **The Unlit Centuries** | ~400 BL to 0 YL | warring human petty-kingdoms, plague, cold | Saint Solenne brings the Brand to the surface |
| **Fourth Age: Age of the Lantern** | 0 YL to now (1187 YL) | the Lanternate and the Solenne Empire | **Now ending.** The Night of Falling Ash |

## First Age: the Age of Iron

- The **Orrin** were giants of living iron and stone that Orr made from the slag of his forge to tend the young world. They were builders without art, endlessly patient.
- They raised the **Wrought Dominion** in the high places of the Ironspine and drove **Nine Nails** of true-iron deep into the earth to pin the Mire where it pressed up hardest. The **Vault of the Nine Nails** still exists, under Korrath.
- **The Rusting.** The Orrin dug toward the Heartfire, wanting to rekindle their dead maker. The heat they released *rusted them from within*. Most froze into the stone statues and ridges that still dot the Ironspine. Their smaller, shorter-lived, more stubborn descendants are the **Orrun** (dwarf-like stone-folk).
- Remnants: iron sentinels that still walk their patrol routes (*the Wrought*), Rustwraiths, and giant works like the **Giant's Sluice** in Aldermoor.

## Second Age: the Age of Leaves

- The **Aelthi** awoke in the forests, "first-born of Kethra" by their own telling. They were long-lived and musical, and their magic was **song**: they shaped ember by harmony.
- Their great court **Ys Aerwen** stood on a lake in what is now the Ylwen Deepwood. They had smaller courts across the west, including a holy grove at what is now **Aldermoor**: the Weeping Stones in the Gloamwood are theirs.
- The Aelthi heard the Dreaming and grieved for it. Their loremasters composed the **Lullaby** (*Hun-Aerwen*), a song to sing the Mire *asleep* instead of keeping it chained.
- **The Sundering of Ys** (~1100 BL). The Lullaby worked too well. The Mire dreamed *closer*, and Ys Aerwen softened and sank into its own lake overnight, along with half of the Aelthi people. Survivors withdrew into **Caer Ylwen** and swore never to sing to the Mire again. A few secretly kept the song. Their descendants are the **Thornwise**.

## Third Age: the Age of Ash

- Humans spread across Morvane from the south-east. The greatest early realm was **Qadir**, ruled by sorcerer-kings who found that *ley-lines could be tapped like wells*.
- The **Ashen Kings** built cities of glass and fire. They bred **cinderdrakes**. Their last king, **Ozrah the Unsleeping**, tried to draw a whole ley-nexus to make himself undying.
- **The Burning of Qadir** (~400 BL). The leys under Qadir overdrew and burned. The land became the **Cinder Wastes**, and the survivors became the nomadic **Qasiri**, who still carry the shame and the craft. The disaster is why every later culture fears unrestrained magic. The Lanternate's licences point straight back to it.

## The Unlit Centuries (~400 BL to 0 YL)

- Cold, plague and petty kings. The ash from Qadir dimmed the sun for a generation (the **Grey Winters**). Human peoples took shape: the Haldish in the west-centre, the Solenni in the south, the Hrothi in the north.
- Folk religion (the **Old Hearth**) dates from this time: keep the fire lit, honour the ancestors, survive the winter.

## Fourth Age: the Age of the Lantern (0 YL to 1187 YL)

| Year (YL) | Event |
|---|---|
| **0** | **Saint Solenne the Bright**, a shepherd-girl turned pilgrim, descends into the deep under the Ironspine and returns with **the Brand**, a living coal of the Heartfire that never goes out. She founds the city of **Solenne** around it. Year 0. |
| 0–200 | The **Lanternate** church forms around the Brand. The **Solenne Empire** spreads north by faith as much as conquest. Brand-lit lanterns are carried to every major city. |
| **112** | The **Emberwardens** are founded: an order of mage-knights sworn to guard the leys, police overdraw and hunt the Mire's seeps. Seat: **Cinderhold**. |
| ~300 | The Orrun of the Ironspine sign the **Iron Concord** with the Empire: trade, not fealty. |
| ~450 | The **Wick-Edict**: all magic outside the Church needs a licence. The **Order of the Wick** is created to enforce it. |
| ~600 | Haldish settlers clear the eastern moors; **Brindlecross** is founded at the old Aelthi crossroads. |
| **702** | The town of **Varn** is chartered on the drained edge of Greywater Marsh. |
| ~780 | The **Abbey of Saint Wenna** is built on an islet in Wennamere. Wenna was an abbess famous for curing marsh-fever by Radiance. |
| **870** | **The Charter of Haldren.** The Haldish kingdoms unite under the first King of Haldmarch, **Edric Barrowborn**, and win self-rule from the Empire. The Church keeps its authority there. |
| **960–978** | **The War of the Two Lanterns.** A schism over whether the Hierophant or the Kings hold authority over the Brand-lanterns. Brackhaven declares itself a free port and the **Salt League** forms. |
| **1041** | **The Purge of the Emberwardens** ("the Night of Snuffed Candles"). The Lanternate, fearing an armed mage order outside its control, declares the Emberwardens heretics. Cinderhold is stormed and its wardens burned or scattered. *A hidden question in the campaign:* the Emberwardens had begun reporting that **the leys were cooling**, and the Church buried those reports. |
| 1100–1160 | A slow chill. The winters lengthen. Hedge-mages report "weak draws". The Church blames sin. |
| **1163** | The **Grey Lung plague**. |
| **1170** | **Ysolde Maerin**, a Lightsworn healer (and secretly the last apprentice of the hiding Emberwarden **Hesta Corrow**), ends the Grey Lung with miraculous Radiance. She is **canonised in her lifetime as Saint Ysolde**. |
| **1178** | Ysolde is allowed to tend the Brand itself as **Keeper of the Brand**. Gazing into it, she begins to have visions of the Dreaming. |
| **1181** | Ysolde vanishes on pilgrimage into the Ironspine, retracing Saint Solenne's path to the Heartforge. She is declared dead. |
| **1185** | The Drowned Choir, an obscure marsh cult, suddenly grows. Hollowed sightings in Greywater. |
| **1186, Lanternmass night** | **THE NIGHT OF FALLING ASH.** Ysolde, now the **Pale Saint**, returns to Solenne cathedral and **quenches the Brand** with black water drawn from the Mire. It shatters into **Seven Embers**, which streak across the sky like falling stars and land across Morvane. Ash falls for three days. **Varn** is flooded and burned as its lord opens the gates to the Choir. Every ley flickers. |
| **1187 (now)** | **The Long Winter.** Spring has not come. The leys are weakening; the Mire seeps upward; the dead are restless; the Church is in crisis and its Inquisition hunts anyone "marked". The Haldmarch's King Aldred died in the winter, leaving a 15-year-old queen and an ambitious regent. **The game begins on the first anniversary of the Night of Falling Ash.** |

## Why the Pale Saint did it (her logic, revealed in Act II)

Ysolde saw through the Brand into the Heartfire, and through the Heartfire into the Dreaming. She learned three things:
1. The Heartfire is **dying anyway**. The leys have cooled for two centuries, and the Emberwardens knew it.
2. When it goes out by itself the world will not gently dream. It will **tear**, slowly and in agony, over a century of famine and Hollowing.
3. If the Heartfire is quenched **deliberately**, by someone who carries all Seven Embers back to it and *gives* them to the Mire, the world will **unforge gently**: everyone will simply fall asleep and dream forever.

She thinks this is mercy. She might be right about (1) and (2). Whether (3) is true is a lie Nhal is telling her, or at best a half-truth. That is the Act III reveal.
