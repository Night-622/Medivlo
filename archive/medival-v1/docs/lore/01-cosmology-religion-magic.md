# 01: Cosmology, Religion, Magic

## 1. The creation myth: "The Forging"

*As told in the Lanternate's* Canticle of the Anvil, *the version most people know:*

> Before the world there was only the **Mire**, a black sea without bottom, shore or shape, in which nothing was and everything might be. Out of the Mire rose **Orr**, who was the first thing to want to be *one* thing. He drew a coal from his own chest and laid it on the anvil of his ribs, and he struck it.
>
> With every blow the world took shape: stone from the first, sea from the second, sky from the third. From every blow a **Spark** flew, and every Spark became a god. Seven blows, seven Sparks. On the eighth blow the hammer slipped, and the Spark that flew fell back into the Mire and was drowned.
>
> When the world was made Orr was spent. He lay down across it, and his bones became the **Ironspine**. His heart, still burning, sank into the deep places beneath the mountains, and there it burns still: the **Heartfire**. Its warmth rises through the world in veins of light, which are the **leys**, and all warmth and all life and all magic is borrowed from it.

### What's actually true (revealed over the campaign)

- The Mire was not empty. It was **dreaming**: a vast, slow, plural mind in which all possible things floated, unmade and unhurt. The Aelthi call it **the Dreaming** (*Ae'Hun*), and some of their oldest songs mourn it.
- Orr did not *make* the world from nothing. He **took it from the Mire by force**, hammering dream into fixed matter. Making the world was also a binding.
- The Heartfire is not just warmth. It is the **nail** that holds the forged world down over the Mire. As long as it burns, the world stays solid. When it cools, the world begins to *unforge* back into dream.
- The Eighth Spark, **Nhal**, did not "fall". Nhal **leapt**. Nhal was the only god who pitied the Mire, and chose to drown with it. Nhal has been down there for every age since, the dreaming sea's single waking thought, and Nhal wants the world returned.
- None of this makes Orr evil, or the Mire good. A world that is *something* is a world in which people can live, love and die. The Dreaming has no suffering, but it has no one in it either. **That is the campaign's central dilemma.**

## 2. The gods: the Seven Sparks (and the Eighth)

Each Spark governs a domain, tints ember into a magic school, and has a holy colour, a symbol and a day of the week. The Haldish week has seven days named for them.

| Spark | Epithet | Domain | Symbol / colour | School | Weekday |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ithren** | the Lantern | light, truth, law, kingship | a lantern; white-gold | Radiance | Ithrensday |
| **Vor** | the Hammer | craft, war, endurance, oaths | a hammer and anvil; iron-grey | Runecraft (crafting) | Vorsday |
| **Saela** | the Tide-Mother | water, cold, mercy, the passage of the dead | a shell or a cupped hand; sea-blue | Rime | Saelsday |
| **Kethra** | the Thorn | wilds, the hunt, growth and rot | an antler crown; green-brown | Verdant | Kethday |
| **Morrow** | the Veiled | death, memory, dreams, secrets | a veiled face or closed eye; grey | Umbral | Morrowday |
| **Ashvar** | the Ember-Tongue | fire, passion, change, rebellion | a flame-tongue; red-orange | Ash | Ashday |
| **Calun** | the Wanderer | wind, storms, travel, luck, tricks | a feather or an open road; storm-violet | Storm | Calunday |
| **Nhal** | the Drowned / the Eighth / the Unnamed | the Mire, unmaking, the Dreaming, pity | a black circle; no colour | Mirecraft (forbidden) | none (the "eighth day" is a heretic's joke) |

Notes:
- **Ithren** is ranked "first among the Seven" only by the Lanternate. Folk religion treats all seven as equal siblings.
- **Saela** ferries the dead down the **River Under** to Morrow's halls. Since the Quench her river is freezing, so **the dead aren't crossing**. That explains the Restless in the Witherdowns.
- **Morrow** and **Saela** are lovers in folk tales; **Ashvar** and **Ithren** are rivals (passion against law).
- Speaking Nhal's name is a crime in the Empire. Cultists say "the Drowned One" or simply "Below".

## 3. Religions and churches

### The Lanternate (Church of the Seven Lights)
- The state church of the **Solenne Empire**, and dominant in the Haldmarch. Seat: the **Cathedral of the Brand** in Solenne. Head: the **Hierophant**.
- Doctrine: the Seven are Orr's children and Ithren is their first-born and lawful heir. The **Brand** was Ithren's gift: a coal of the Heartfire carried to the surface by **Saint Solenne the Bright** in 0 YL, proof that the Church holds the world's light. All magic is the gods' property; mortals may use it only **under licence**, a *Wick-licence*.
- Clergy: hierarchs, priests ("Brother/Sister"), **Lightsworn** (holy knights; Oathbound class), and the **Order of the Wick** (inquisitors and witchfinders, who "trim the wick so the flame burns true").
- Rites: the **Lanternmass** (midwinter festival of light); lamp vigils for the dead; the oath "By the Seventh Light".
- Since the Quench the Church is in a **legitimacy crisis**. If the Brand was Ithren's favour, what does its going dark mean?

### The Old Hearth (folk faith)
- The village religion of the Haldmarch and the north. All seven gods are equal; ancestors are honoured at the household hearth. **Hearthwardens** are lay priests, often old women.
- The Lanternate tolerates it, while trying to fold its festivals into the church calendar.
- Rites: the **remembrance pyre** (a bonfire on the anniversary of a disaster); hearth-ash smeared on doorposts for protection; never letting the house fire go out in winter.

### The Thornwise (druidic)
- Kethra-centred nature faith of the Ylwen Deepwood, held by the Aelthi and by human hedge-witches. They believe Orr's Forging was necessary but cruel, and that the Mire deserves **mourning, not hatred**.
- They sing the old Aelthi songs to keep the Mire *asleep* rather than chained.

### The Grey Monks of Morrow
- A cloistered order that keeps the dead: ossuaries, archives of memory, funeral rites. Politically neutral. They know more about the Mire than any other order, because dreams and the Mire are related, and they keep that knowledge secret.

### The Ashen Creed (heretical)
- Ashvar-centred. Believes magic belongs to everyone and that the Lanternate's licences are tyranny. Some members think Ashvar is the true heir, not Ithren. They are hunted by the Wick. Many Qasiri hold a milder form of this faith.

### The Drowned Choir (forbidden cult)
- Worships Nhal and the Mire. Its creed: *"The world is a wound. The Forging was the blade. Let the water close over it, and let all things dream again."*
- Hymns in the Drowned Tongue. Rituals of drowning and "baptism in black water". Members become **Hollowed** willingly. Since the Quench they've grown from a hidden cult into an army.

## 4. How magic works

### Ember and the leys
- The Heartfire's heat rises through **leys**, veins of warmth in rock, water and living things. Where leys run close to the surface you get hot springs, glowing veins in stone, lush growth in winter, and holy sites.
- A mage is someone whose body can **draw** that heat and shape it. The drawn heat is called **ember** (the in-game mana resource: *Ember*).
- The **Seven Sparks tint ember**. A mage attuned to Ashvar's tint makes fire; to Saela's, cold and water; and so on. Most mages can manage one or two tints. That is why there are **schools**.
- **Spell tomes** contain *tint-patterns*, diagrams and chants that teach the body a new way to shape ember. That is why spells are learned from books found in the world.
- **Overdraw** pulls more ember than the body can carry, and it scars: **cinderlung** (coughing ash), burn-lines on the skin, and eventually the mage burns out to ash. The Ashen Kings of Qadir did this to a whole land.

### The schools (player-facing; Claude 3 owns mechanics)
| School | Spark | Lore flavour | Who teaches it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ash** | Ashvar | fire is change; it consumes so new things grow | the Ashen Creed, Qasiri flame-singers, hedge-mages |
| **Rime** | Saela | cold is stillness and mercy; it numbs pain, preserves, ends | Saela's tide-priests (Saltreach), hedge-witches |
| **Storm** | Calun | the sky's restlessness; luck, speed, lightning | Hrothi storm-callers, Salt League weather-witches |
| **Radiance** | Ithren | ordered light; wards, truth, healing, smiting the Hollowed | the Lanternate only (legally) |
| **Umbral** | Morrow | shadow is memory's shape; curses, fear, speaking with the dead | Grey Monks; feared by the Church, tolerated barely |
| **Verdant** | Kethra | growth and rot are one cycle; roots, poison, beasts | the Thornwise and the Aelthi |
| *Runecraft* | Vor | ember fixed into matter; enchanting, runeforging, upgrades | the Orrun (the crafting system, not a spell school) |
| *Mirecraft* | Nhal | **not** ember: draws on the *caster's own warmth and life*, and the Mire answers | the Drowned Choir (enemy magic) |

### Bloodcraft (Reaver class)
- Drawing ember from **your own body's warmth** instead of the leys. Powerful, fast, costly. Lanternate law calls it the *first step to Mirecraft*, and the law is right: life-warmth is exactly what the Mire answers to. Reavers walk a line. The Choir recruits them, and the Church hangs them.
- As the leys fail through the campaign, Bloodcraft (and worse) becomes **tempting for everyone**.

### The Ashmark (the player's unique power)
- A splinter of a falling Ember scarred the player's hand. The Ashmark is a **direct thread to the Heartfire** that bypasses the leys.
- Abilities that grow over the acts: **Kindle** (light a dark brazier or ward; the tutorial), **Purge** (burn Mire-taint out of a person, beast or place; damages Hollowed and Unwrought; can cleanse instead of kill), **Embersight** (see leys, hidden Mire seeps and lore traces), and late-game **Carry** (hold Embers; each Ember you carry adds power and visions of the Pale Saint).
- **Cost:** Purge burns. Overusing it on people can kill them. This drives the prologue's Tobin choice.
